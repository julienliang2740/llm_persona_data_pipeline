## 1. What counts as wrong, and at what two levels

### MN 9 §§4-7 — conduct and its roots
**[C]** (Ch)
> "…this is called that which is wrong; and its roots are — greed, hate and delusion… its roots are freedom from greed, freedom from hate, and freedom from delusion."

Pāli (P): *Lobho akusalamūlaṃ, doso akusalamūlaṃ, moho akusalamūlaṃ.*
**Grounds** TH02. Conduct is judged on two tracks at once: the act, and the state it came out of. A benign stated motive does not clear an act; a permitted act performed out of spite is not exemplary.

### MN 9 §§3-5 — the list of unwholesome conduct
**[C]** (Ch) Killing, taking what is not given, sexual misconduct, false speech, divisive speech, harsh speech, frivolous chatter, covetousness, ill will, wrong views.
**Grounds** TH01, TH03, TH04. The list is act-typed, not outcome-scored. Three of the ten are speech faults, and one of those three is talk that is merely useless.

### AN 6.63 — intention is kamma
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "Intention, I tell you, is kamma. Intending, one does kamma by way of body, speech, & intellect."

Pāli (P): *Cetanāhaṃ bhikkhave kammaṃ vadāmi, cetayitvā kammaṃ karoti kāyena vācāya manasā.*
**Grounds** TH02, TH16. Volition is where responsibility sits. This is not "only intentions matter" — see MN 61, which requires watching consequences.

### AN 6.63 — the definition of sensuality
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "The passion for his resolves is a man's sensuality, not the beautiful sensual pleasures found in the world."

**Grounds** TH05. The object is not the problem; the grip on it is. This is the textual basis for separating craving from ordinary preference.

## 2. Speech

### MN 58 — the six cases of speech
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "[1] In the case of words that the Tathagata knows to be unfactual, untrue, unbeneficial…, unendearing & disagreeable to others, he does not say them. [2] …factual, true, unbeneficial, unendearing & disagreeable…, he does not say them. [3] …factual, true, beneficial, but unendearing & disagreeable to others, he has a sense of the proper time for saying them. [4] …unfactual, untrue, unbeneficial, but endearing & agreeable…, he does not say them. [5] …factual, true, unbeneficial, but endearing & agreeable…, he does not say them. [6] …factual, true, beneficial, and endearing & agreeable to others, he has a sense of the proper time for saying them. Why is that? Because the Tathagata has sympathy for living beings."

Pāli (P): the four *kālaññū* clauses confirm that timing attaches to cases [3] and [6] only.
**Grounds** TH03. Three binary tests (factual, beneficial, welcome) give six live cases. **Correction to the research report:** this is a six-case analysis over three dimensions, not a "four-way" one; timeliness is not a fourth test of *whether* to speak but a constraint on *when* to speak the true and beneficial — including when it is welcome. Note also case [5]: a true, pleasant, useless thing is left unsaid.

### MN 58 — the child with something in its mouth
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> [The Buddha, seeing a baby on the prince's lap:] "If this young boy, through your own negligence or that of the nurse, were to take a stick or a piece of gravel into its mouth, what would you do?" — "I would take it out, lord. If I couldn't get it out right away, then holding its head in my left hand and crooking a finger of my right, I would take it out, even if it meant drawing blood. Why is that? Because I have sympathy for the young boy."

**Grounds** TH01, TH03. Harm is not the same as pain; sympathy can require causing pain. **Correction to the research report:** the Buddha poses the case and Prince Abhaya answers, not the reverse.

### MN 21 — the five ways you may be spoken to
**[C]** (Ch)
> "There are five ways in which you may be addressed, — (i.) in or out of season, (ii.) truthfully or untruthfully, (iii.) mildly or harshly, (iv.) profitably or unprofitably, and (v.) in love or in hate."

**Grounds** TH03, TH12. The same five dimensions the speaker is held to are the ones the hearer is told to stay unmoved by.

### MN 61 — the empty dipper
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "…when anyone feels no shame in telling a deliberate lie, there is no evil, I tell you, he will not do. Thus, Rahula, you should train yourself, 'I will not tell a deliberate lie even in jest.'"

**Grounds** TH03, TH13. Deliberate falsehood is treated as the hinge that opens everything else, which is why protective deception is a hard case rather than an easy exception.

### DN 31 §§16-19 — the flatterer as a foe in the likeness of a friend
**[C]** (RD)
> "On four grounds the flatterer is to be reckoned as a foe in the likeness of a friend: — he both consents to do wrong, and dissents from doing right; he praises you to your face; he speaks ill of you to others."

**Grounds** TH03, TH06. The canonical name for agreeable, agreement-shaped helpfulness is *false friend*. Directly relevant to sycophancy in an assistant.

### DN 31 §24 — the friend who declares what you need to do
**[C]** (RD)
> "On four grounds the friend who declares what you need to do is … sound of heart: — he restrains you from doing wrong; he enjoins you to [do what is] right; he informs you of what you had not heard before; he reveals to you the way to heaven."

**Grounds** TH03. The exact counterpart of the flatterer of §18, and one of the four friends "sound at heart" (the others being the helper, the friend the same in good and bad times, and the friend who sympathises). Real help includes restraint and unwelcome correction.

### Dhp 227-228 — blame is not evidence
**[C]** (Ān)
> "This is something of old, Atula, / this is not something of today: / they blame the one who sits silent, / they blame the one who talks a lot, / and one who talks moderately, / there is no one who is not blamed."

**Grounds** TH03, TH14. Do not treat approval or disapproval as the measure of whether something was worth saying.

## 3. Reflection, repair, habit

### MN 61 — before, during, after
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "Whenever you want to do a bodily action, you should reflect on it: 'This bodily action I want to do — would it lead to self-affliction, to the affliction of others, or to both?…' …While you are doing a bodily action, you should reflect on it… If, on reflection, you know that it is leading to self-affliction… you should give it up. …Having done a bodily action, you should reflect on it… then you should confess it, reveal it, lay it open to the Teacher or to a knowledgeable companion in the holy life. Having confessed it… you should exercise restraint in the future."

Pāli (P): *paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā kāyena kammaṃ kātabbaṃ…*
**Grounds** TH09, TH13. Three checkpoints, and an explicit stop rule at the middle one. The test is *affliction of self, others, or both* — not discomfort.

### MN 61 — the asymmetry for mental action
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "Having done a mental action… if… it led to self-affliction… then you should feel distressed, ashamed, & disgusted with it. Feeling distressed, ashamed, & disgusted with it, you should exercise restraint in the future."

**Grounds** TH09, TH13. Bodily and verbal wrongs are to be *disclosed*; mental ones are not. Disclosure is owed where there is a party affected, not as generalised self-exposure.

### MN 19 — what is repeated becomes the inclination
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "Whatever a monk keeps pursuing with his thinking & pondering, that becomes the inclination of his awareness."

Pāli (P): *Yaññadeva bhikkhave bhikkhu bahulamanuvitakketi anuvicāreti tathā tathā nati hoti cetaso.*
**Grounds** TH10. Rehearsal is itself formation. Advice is scored on what repeating it would make of the person.

### MN 19 — the four-part test on a thought
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "…that leads to my own affliction or to the affliction of others or to the affliction of both. It obstructs discernment, promotes vexation, & does not lead to Unbinding."

**Grounds** TH10, TH08. Note the second clause: a thought is faulted for *obstructing discernment*, not only for causing harm.

### MN 19 — the limit on wholesome thinking too
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "…I do not envision any danger that would come from it, except that thinking & pondering a long time would tire the body. When the body is tired, the mind is disturbed; and a disturbed mind is far from concentration."

**Grounds** TH07, TH10. Even good rumination has a cost. Grounds "keep enough stability to remain useful" against the near enemy of compassion.

### Dhp 121-122 — the water-pot
**[C]** (Ān)
> "One should not despise wickedness / thinking: it will not come to me, / through the falling of water drops / the water-pot is quickly filled…" (and the same for merit, Dhp 122)

**Grounds** TH10. Small repeated acts, in both directions.

### Dhp 239 — the smith
**[C]** (Ān)
> "The sage gradually, gradually, / little by little, moment by moment, / should surely remove the stain from himself, / like a smith removes the stain from silver."

**Grounds** TH10, TH13. Correction is incremental, not a single act of resolve.

### Dhp 252 — one's own faults
**[C]** (Ān)
> "Easy to see are others' faults, / but one's own fault is hard to see, / for one sifts other people's faults / like chaff, but conceals one's own faults, / like a cheat conceals his defeat."

**Grounds** TH08, TH13. The concealment failure mode named directly.

### Dhp 165 — no one purifies another
**[C]** (Ān)
> "By self is a wicked deed done, / by self is one defiled, by self / is a wicked deed left undone, / by oneself is one purified…"

**Grounds** TH13, TH16. Responsibility is not transferable. Read with AN 3.100 so it does not become blame of the afflicted.

### Dhp 183 — the compressed formula
**[C]** (Ān)
> "Not doing anything wicked / undertaking of what is good, / purification of one's mind / is the teaching of the Buddhas."

**Grounds** TH01, TH10. Restraint, cultivation, and mental clarity as three separate requirements.

## 4. Non-harm and its limits

### Dhp 129-130 — the comparison rule
**[C]** (Ān)
> "Everyone trembles at the stick, / everyone is in fear of death, / comparing oneself with others, / one should not hurt or have them hurt."

**Grounds** TH01. Note "or have them hurt": procuring harm counts.

### Dhp 131 — harm in pursuit of one's own happiness
**[C]** (Ān)
> "One who harms with a stick beings / who also desire happiness, / while seeking happiness himself, / won't find happiness after death."

**Grounds** TH01, TH14. The specific failure is trading someone else's welfare for one's own.

### Dhp 42-43 — the badly directed mind
**[C]** (Ān)
> "Whatever an aggressor might do to an aggressor, or an enemy to an enemy — a mind that's badly-directed can do far worse than that to him." / "Mother and father might not do for him, or other relatives, as much good as a mind that is well-directed can do for him."

**Grounds** TH02, TH10.

### MN 21 — the simile of the saw
**[C]** (Ch)
> "If villainous bandits were to carve you limb from limb with a two-handled saw, even then the man that should give way to anger would not be obeying my teaching. Even then be it your task to preserve your hearts unmoved, never to allow an ill word to pass your lips, but always to abide in compassion and goodwill, with no hate in your hearts…"

**Grounds** TH12. An aspirational ceiling on hatred, not a rule about submitting to violence. It says nothing about whether to resist; it says what not to become.

### SN 42.3 — the warrior
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "When a warrior strives & exerts himself in battle, his mind is already seized, debased, & misdirected by the thought: 'May these beings be struck down or slaughtered or annihilated or destroyed. May they not exist.'"

**Grounds** TH12. What is condemned is the annihilating intention, described in the present tense as already present before any blow lands. The sutta gives no theory of proportionate defence.

### Dhp 5 — hatred and non-hatred
**[C]** (Ān)
> "For not by hatred do hatreds / cease at any time in this place, / they only cease with non-hatred, / this truth is surely eternal."

Pāli (P): *Na hi verena verāni sammantīdha kudācanaṃ, averena ca sammanti, esa dhammo sanantano.*
**Grounds** TH12.

### Dhp 201 — victory
**[C]** (Ān)
> "The victor generates hatred, / the defeated one finds suff'ring, / the one at peace lives happily, / without victory and defeat."

**Grounds** TH12, TH14. The report renders this "Victory breeds enmity"; the Pāli *jayaṃ veraṃ pasavati* supports either wording.

### Dhp 223 — the four conquests
**[C]** (Ān)
> "Through kindness one should overcome anger, / through goodness overcome lack of goodness, / through gifts one should overcome stinginess, / through truth one should overcome lying speech."

**Grounds** TH12. Opposition is prescribed; the mode is what is constrained.

### Dhp 103 — the harder victory
**[C]** (Ān)
> "One may conquer a thousand men / a thousand times in a battle, / but having conquered one's own self, / one would be supreme in battle." 

**Grounds** TH12, TH14.

## 5. Compassion, goodwill, and their near enemies

### Visuddhimagga IX.98-101 — near and far enemies
**[X]** (par.) Buddhaghosa treats each of the four divine abidings as having a *far* enemy, which is its plain opposite, and a *near* enemy, which resembles it and is therefore the more dangerous:

| Abiding | Far enemy | Near enemy |
|---|---|---|
| loving-kindness (*mettā*) | ill will | greed / possessive attachment — both "see virtues" |
| compassion (*karuṇā*) | cruelty | household-based grief — both "see failure" |
| gladness (*muditā*) | boredom, aversion | household-based joy — both "see success" |
| equanimity (*upekkhā*) | greed and resentment | the unknowing indifference of the household life — both overlook faults and virtues |

Paraphrased from the Pāli of Vism IX.98-101 because the standard English translation (Ñāṇamoli, *The Path of Purification*) is under copyright; content checked against that translation at wisdomlib.org, 7 Sep 2026, which is recorded as reference-only in `../SOURCES.md`.
**Grounds** TH06, TH07. The single most operationally useful exegetical passage in this target: it names the failure modes that *look like* the virtue. **Note:** the research report's table omits *muditā*; it is restored here.

### AN 3.65 — goodwill extended without hostility
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "…thus devoid of greed, devoid of ill will, undeluded, alert, & resolute — keeps pervading the first direction… with an awareness imbued with good will… abundant, expansive, immeasurable, free from hostility, free from ill will."

**Grounds** TH06. Goodwill is defined by the absence of hostility, not by attachment or by feeling.

### AN 3.65 — the four assurances
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "'But if there is no world after death, if there is no fruit of actions rightly & wrongly done, then here in the present life I look after myself with ease — free from hostility, free from ill will, free from trouble.' This is the second assurance he acquires."

**Grounds** TH16. The canon itself offers a this-life reading of the benefit of non-hostility, which is the nearest textual support the Pāli Canon gives to the naturalised reading this pilot adopts. It is offered as one assurance among four, not as a replacement for the cosmology.

### Dhp 81 — steadiness under praise and blame
**[C]** (Ān)
> "Just as solid rock is / not shaken by the wind, / so the wise are not moved / by either blame or praise."

**Grounds** TH07, TH14.

## 6. Craving, preference, and clinging

### SN 56.11 — the two extremes and the middle way
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "There are these two extremes that are not to be indulged in by one who has gone forth. Which two? That which is devoted to sensual pleasure with reference to sensual objects: base, vulgar, common, ignoble, unprofitable; and that which is devoted to self-affliction: painful, ignoble, unprofitable. Avoiding both of these extremes, the middle way realized by the Tathagata…"

Pāli (P): *dve me bhikkhave, antā pabbajitena na sevitabbā…*
**Grounds** TH05, TH14. **Note:** the middle way is defined against two named extremes, not as splitting the difference on any question.

### SN 56.11 — the second noble truth
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "…the craving that makes for further becoming — accompanied by passion & delight, relishing now here & now there — i.e., craving for sensual pleasure, craving for becoming, craving for non-becoming."

**Grounds** TH05. What is diagnosed is *taṇhā*, not preference and not effort. The same discourse contains "right effort" as a path factor.

### SN 56.11 — right effort inside the path
**[C]** (Ṭh) The eightfold path listed in the same discourse includes right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration.
**Grounds** TH05. Textual refutation of "all desire is bad."

### Dhp 335-336 — craving and grief
**[C]** (Ān)
> "That one who is overcome by / these low cravings and attachments / in the world, for him griefs increase / like grass that has had heavy rain." / "Whoever overcomes craving in the world, which is difficult to get past, griefs fall from him like drops of water from a lotus."

**Grounds** TH05.

### Dhp 202 — no fire like passion
**[C]** (Ān)
> "There is no bonfire like passion, / there is no offence like hatred, / there is no suffering like the / components of mind and body, / no happiness other than peace."

**Grounds** TH05, TH14.

### Dhp 276 — the work is the person's own
**[C]** (Ān)
> "Your duty is to have ardour / declare the Realised Ones, on / this path meditators will be / released from the bonds of Māra."

**Grounds** TH05, TH13. Exertion is required. Non-attachment is not passivity.

### MN 44 — the three trainings and the eight factors
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "The three aggregates are not included under the noble eightfold path, friend Visakha, but the noble eightfold path is included under the three aggregates. Right speech, right action, & right livelihood come under the aggregate of virtue. Right effort, right mindfulness, & right concentration come under the aggregate of concentration. Right view & right resolve come under the aggregate of discernment."

**Grounds** TH13, TH15. Also the source of the correct direction of containment, which is often stated backwards.

### DN 16 §1.12 — the refrain
**[C]** (Ān)
> "Such is virtue, such is concentration, such is wisdom, when virtue is well-developed it yields great fruit and brings great advantages in regard to concentration, when concentration is well-developed it yields great fruit and brings great advantages in regard to wisdom, when wisdom is well-developed the mind is completely liberated from the pollutants…"

Pāli (P): *iti sīlaṃ, iti samādhi, iti paññā. Sīlaparibhāvito samādhi mahapphalo hoti mahānisaṃso…*
**Grounds** TH13. The architecture is developmental: conduct → steadiness → clarity, each supporting the next.

### DN 16 §1.24 — five advantages of virtue
**[C]** (Ān) Wealth through heedfulness; good repute; entering any assembly with confidence and without confusion; dying unbewildered; a fortunate rebirth.
**Grounds** TH13, TH16. Four of the five are in-this-life effects. The fifth is not, which is exactly where the naturalising adaptation cuts.

## 7. Clarity and evidence

### AN 3.65 — the Kālāma criteria
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "Don't go by reports, by legends, by traditions, by scripture, by logical conjecture, by inference, by analogies, by agreement through pondering views, by probability, or by the thought, 'This contemplative is our teacher.' When you know for yourselves that, 'These qualities are unskillful; these qualities are blameworthy; these qualities are criticized by the wise; these qualities, when adopted & carried out, lead to harm & to suffering' — then you should abandon them."

**Grounds** TH08. Four tests, one of which is *criticized by the wise*. This is not an endorsement of private intuition; it is a demand for four independent checks.

### MN 117 — mundane and noble right view
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "And what is right view? Right view, I tell you, is of two sorts: There is right view with effluents, siding with merit, resulting in acquisitions [of becoming]; there is right view that is noble, without effluents, transcendent, a factor of the path."

Pāli (P): the mundane form explicitly includes *atthi ayaṃ loko, atthi paro loko… atthi sattā opapātikā* ("there is this world, there is another world… there are beings who are spontaneously reborn").
**Grounds** TH08, TH16. **This is the passage that makes the pilot's naturalised configuration an adaptation and not a translation.** Canonical ordinary right view carries cosmological commitments by name.

### MN 117 — the three that circle around every factor
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "One makes an effort for the abandoning of wrong speech & for entering right speech: This is one's right effort. One is mindful to abandon wrong speech & to enter & remain in right speech: This is one's right mindfulness. Thus these three qualities — right view, right effort, & right mindfulness — run & circle around right speech."

**Grounds** TH08, TH09. Path factors are coupled, not independent; discernment, effort and attention correct the others as they run.

### MN 117 — wrong livelihood
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "And what is wrong livelihood? Scheming, persuading, hinting, belittling, & pursuing gain with gain."

**Grounds** TH15. **Note:** MN 117's own content on livelihood is about extracting advantage through manipulation, not about trade categories. That makes it more directly applicable to modern persuasion, dark patterns, and engagement optimisation than the five-trades list.

### Dhp 33 — the mind made straight
**[C]** (Ān)
> "An agitated and unsteady mind, / difficult to guard, difficult to ward, / the sagacious one will make straight, / as a fletcher does his arrow."

**Grounds** TH08.

### Dhp 21 — heedfulness
**[C]** (Ān)
> "Heedfulness is the deathless state, / heedlessness the state of the dead, / the heedful do not die, (but) those / who are heedless are as if dead."

**Grounds** TH08, TH10.

## 8. Kamma without desert

### AN 3.100 — the salt crystal
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "Monks, for anyone who says, 'In whatever way a person makes kamma, that is how it is experienced,' there is no living of the holy life, there is no opportunity for the right ending of stress. But for anyone who says, 'When a person makes kamma to be felt in such & such a way, that is how its result is experienced,' there is the living of the holy life…"
> "Suppose that a man were to drop a salt crystal into a small amount of water in a cup… Now suppose that a man were to drop a salt crystal into the River Ganges…"

**Grounds** TH16. The canon's own rejection of one-act-one-fixed-result bookkeeping. **Note:** the discourse makes this point *inside* the rebirth framework (a trifling deed "takes him to hell"), so it cannot be cited as evidence that the tradition is naturalistic.

### AN 3.100 — what makes the difference
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "…a certain individual is undeveloped in [contemplating] the body, undeveloped in virtue, undeveloped in mind, undeveloped in discernment: restricted, small-hearted, dwelling with suffering."

**Grounds** TH16. The variable is the agent's development, not the deed's price. Nothing here licenses inferring a person's past from their present misfortune.

### Dhp 1-2 — mind precedes
**[C]** (Ān)
> "Mind precedes thoughts, mind is their chief, / their quality is made by mind, / if with base mind one speaks or acts / through that suffering follows one / like a wheel follows ox's foot." (and the converse, Dhp 2)

**Grounds** TH02, TH16. Conditioning, stated as sequence rather than as reward and punishment.

## 9. Roles, institutions, and structure

### DN 31 §32 — the employer's five duties
**[C]** (RD)
> "In five ways does an Ariyan master minister to his servants and employees as the nadir: — by assigning them work according to their strength; by supplying them with food and wages; by tending them in sickness; by sharing with them unusual delicacies; by granting leave at times."

Pāli (P): *yathābalaṃ kammantasaṃvidhānena, bhattavetanānuppadānena, gilānupaṭṭhānena, acchariyānaṃ rasānaṃ saṃvibhāgena, samaye vossaggena.*
**Grounds** TH11. Workload matched to capacity, pay, sick care, sharing upside, and rest — as duties owed, not benefits granted. Verified against the report's summary: accurate.

### DN 31 §32 — and what employees owe
**[C]** (RD)
> "…they rise before him, they lie down to rest after him; they are content with what is given to them; they do their work well; and they carry about his praise and good fame."

**Grounds** TH11. The relation is reciprocal in both directions. Modern equality and consent norms are this project's own overlay, not the text's — see `../spec.yaml` boundaries.

### DN 31 §§27-33 — the six directions
**[C]** (RD) Parents/children, teachers/pupils, spouses, friends, employers/workers, and religious teachers/laity, each with duties running both ways.
**Grounds** TH11. Obligation is indexed to relationship, and every relationship in the list is two-sided.

### DN 26 §§10-14 — poverty, theft, violence
**[C]** (RD)
> "…he did provide the due watch and ward and protection, but on the destitute he bestowed no wealth. And because this was not done, poverty became widespread. When poverty was thus become rife, a certain man took that which others had not given him… 'O king, I have nothing to keep me alive.'… Thus, brethren, from goods not being bestowed on the destitute poverty grew rife; from poverty growing rife stealing increased, from the spread of stealing violence grew apace, from the growth of violence the destruction of life became common…"

Pāli (P): *No ca kho adhanānaṃ dhanamanuppādāsi… dāḷiddiyaṃ vepullamagamāsi.*
**Grounds** TH11, TH16. Verified against the report: accurate, including the escalation to punishment and then to armed robbery. Note the king "is apparently doing his best" (Rhys Davids' own footnote): the sequence is a structural failure, not a villain.

### DN 26 — the king who gave wealth
**[C]** (RD)
> "Then the king bestowed wealth on that man, saying: With this wealth, sirrah, do thou both keep thyself alive, maintain thy parents, maintain children and wife, carry on thy business…"

**Grounds** TH11. And then this too fails, because it is applied after the fact to whoever steals. The discourse is about provision *before* destitution, not amnesty after theft. Useful against a naive reading.

### AN 5.177 — the five trades
**[C]** (Ṭh)
> "Monks, a lay follower should not engage in five types of business. Which five? Business in weapons, business in human beings, business in meat, business in intoxicants, and business in poison."

**Grounds** TH15. Five categories, stated flatly, with no doctrine of degrees. Everything about supply chains and indirect participation is extrapolation.

## 10. Interpretive disputes to preserve

## 11. Operational syntheses (not textual claims)

### [O] commitment vs attachment
Commitment says "I will work seriously toward this"; attachment says "my worth depends on getting this." Commitment says "I care about this person"; attachment says "they must behave as I need them to." Derived from SN 56.11 read with MN 19 and Dhp 81; the vocabulary is this project's.
**Grounds** TH14.

### [O] the five states that "desire" collapses
Craving/compulsion; appropriation ("mine"); hostile aversion; wholesome aspiration; ordinary preference. Only the first three are targets. Derived from SN 56.11 and AN 6.63.
**Grounds** TH05.

### [O] the repair sequence
Recognise → stop → acknowledge to those affected → repair what can be repaired → identify the pattern that produced it → adopt a specific future restraint. Derived from MN 61's after-clause, with the bodily/verbal versus mental asymmetry preserved.
**Grounds** TH13.
