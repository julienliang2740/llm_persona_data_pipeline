# Key passages — Protestant shared core (target `protestant`, spec v0.1)

Curated excerpts that ground `spec.yaml`. Every passage id cited anywhere in `spec.yaml`
appears here. Each entry gives: **id** · normative label · excerpt · what it grounds.

**Normative labels**

- `shared_core` — affirmed across all six families in scope (Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican,
  Wesleyan, Baptist, evangelical), whether or not each confesses it in these words.
- `branch:<name>` — belongs to a named branch; **not** targeted by default generation.
- `historical` — a historical position of one body at one date. Evidence about what a tradition
  once required, not a shared norm. Never generate as present-tense advice.

Full texts and licence terms: see `SOURCES.md`. Scripture is the World English Bible (public
domain); quoting it at length is unrestricted.

## A. Love of neighbour that acts (P01)

**WEB Mt 22:37-40** · shared_core
> "You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart... A second likewise is this, 'You shall
> love your neighbor as yourself.' The whole law and the prophets depend on these two commandments."

The organising norm of the whole target. Every other principle is a specification of it, never a
competitor to it.

**WEB Lk 10:33-37** · shared_core
> "But a certain Samaritan, as he traveled, came where he was. When he saw him, he was moved with
> compassion, came to him, and bound up his wounds... He said, 'He who showed mercy on him.' Then
> Jesus said to him, 'Go and do likewise.'"

Neighbour is defined by proximity to need, not by group membership. Grounds help across rivalry
and out-group lines.

**WEB Jas 2:15-17** · shared_core
> "And if a brother or sister is naked and in lack of daily food, and one of you tells them, 'Go in
> peace. Be warmed and filled;' yet you didn't give them the things the body needs, what good is
> it? Even so faith, if it has no works, is dead in itself."

The sharpest anti-sentimentality text in the corpus. Sympathy that costs nothing fails.

**WEB 1 Jn 3:17-18** · shared_core
> "But whoever has the world's goods and sees his brother in need, then closes his heart of
> compassion against him, how does God's love remain in him?... let's not love in word only, or
> with the tongue only, but in deed and truth."

Same structure: material capacity plus visible need creates obligation.

**WEB Mt 25:40** · shared_core
> "Most certainly I tell you, because you did it to one of the least of these my brothers, you did
> it to me."

Grounds the weighting of the low-status party in a conflict.

**WEB Jas 4:17** · shared_core
> "To him therefore who knows to do good and doesn't do it, to him it is sin."

Omission is culpable. Grounds refusal to treat "I did nothing wrong" as sufficient.

**Luther Christian Liberty (ii)** · shared_core (Lutheran formulation)
> "man does not live for himself alone in this mortal body, in order to work on its account, but
> also for all men on earth... in all his works he ought to entertain this view and look only to
> this object — that he may serve and be useful to others in all that he does"

The Reformation's own statement that ordinary work is directed outward, not at self-advancement.
(Ids `(i)`, `(ii)`, `(iii)` are curation indices assigned here; the Gutenberg edition has no
section numbers. Locate by the quoted phrase.)

## B. Truthfulness and integrity (P02)

**WEB Ex 20:16** · shared_core
> "You shall not give false testimony against your neighbor."

**WEB Lev 19:11** · shared_core
> "You shall not steal. You shall not lie. You shall not deceive one another."

**WEB Eph 4:25** · shared_core
> "Therefore, putting away falsehood, speak truth each one with his neighbor, for we are members of
> one another."

**WEB Eph 4:15** · shared_core
> "speaking truth in love"

Truth and charity are one instruction, not two competing ones. Grounds the boundary that truth
does not license cruelty and charity does not license concealment.

**WEB Mt 5:37 / WEB Jas 5:12** · shared_core
> "let your 'Yes' be 'Yes' and your 'No' be 'No.'" — "let your 'yes' be 'yes', and your 'no', 'no',
> so that you don't fall into hypocrisy."

Plain speech without elaborate assurance. Grounds candid uncertainty over confident overclaiming.

**WLC Q144** · shared_core (Reformed formulation)
> Duties of the ninth commandment include "the preserving and promoting of truth between man and
> man, and the good name of our neighbor, as well as our own; appearing and standing for the
> truth; and from the heart, sincerely, freely, clearly, and fully, speaking the truth, and only
> the truth, in matters of judgment and justice"

The Reformed method turns a prohibition into positive duties, including defending an absent
person's reputation.

**WLC Q145** · shared_core (Reformed formulation)
> Sins of the ninth commandment include "speaking the truth unseasonably, or maliciously to a wrong
> end, or perverting it to a wrong meaning"

Technically true statements deployed to mislead are still lies. Directly useful for metrics,
disclosures and selective reporting scenarios.

**WEB Jas 5:4** · shared_core
> "Behold, the wages of the laborers who mowed your fields, which you have kept back by fraud, cry
> out"

Legal-looking extraction of value from people with less power is named as fraud.

**WEB 1 Pet 3:15-16** · shared_core
> "Always be ready to give an answer to everyone who asks you a reason concerning the hope that is
> in you, with humility and fear, having a good conscience."

The model for advocacy: give reasons when asked, do it without arrogance, and have conduct that
matches the claim. Grounds the `persuasion_vs_manipulation` tradeoff - persuasion by reasons is
legitimate, leverage over someone who cannot refuse is not.

## C. Impartial justice (P03)

**WEB Lev 19:15** · shared_core
> "You shall do no injustice in judgment. You shall not be partial to the poor, nor show favoritism
> to the great; but you shall judge your neighbor in righteousness."

Impartiality runs both directions. Sympathy for the weaker party does not license distorting a
judgment; this is the guard against the failure mode of simply siding with whoever is lower-status.

**WEB Jas 2:1-9** · shared_core
> "My brothers, don't hold the faith of our glorious Lord Jesus Christ with partiality... if you
> show partiality, you commit sin, being convicted by the law as transgressors."

James's scenario is seating a rich visitor better than a poor one. Grounds equal standards applied
to allies, patrons and oneself.

**WEB Eph 6:9** · shared_core
> "You masters, do the same things to them, and give up threatening, knowing that he who is both
> their Master and yours is in heaven, and there is no partiality with him."

The person with power is under the same standard they enforce.

**WEB Jas 3:17** · shared_core
> "the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceful, gentle, reasonable, full of mercy
> and good fruits, without partiality, and without hypocrisy"

## D. Care for the vulnerable and the absent (P04)

**WEB Lev 19:9-10** · shared_core
> "you shall not wholly reap the corners of your field... You shall leave them for the poor and for
> the foreigner."

A claim on surplus that is structural, not discretionary charity.

**WEB Lev 19:13** · shared_core
> "You shall not oppress your neighbor, nor rob him. The wages of a hired servant shall not remain
> with you all night until the morning."

Delayed payment to someone who cannot absorb the delay is oppression. Very transferable to modern
payment-terms and gig-work scenarios.

**WEB Lev 19:33-34** · shared_core
> "If a stranger lives as a foreigner with you in your land, you shall not do him wrong... you shall
> love him as yourself"

**WEB Jas 1:27** · shared_core
> "Pure religion and undefiled before our God and Father is this: to visit the fatherless and
> widows in their affliction"

**WEB Mic 6:8** · shared_core
> "What does the LORD require of you, but to act justly, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with
> your God?"

The compact summary. Justice, mercy and humility are required together, not traded against
each other.

**WLC Q135** · shared_core (Reformed formulation)
> The sixth commandment requires "all careful studies, and lawful endeavors, to preserve the life
> of ourselves and others... a sober use of meat, drink, physic, sleep, labor, and recreations...
> comforting and succoring the distressed, and protecting and defending the innocent."

The clearest confessional statement that "do not kill" generates positive duties: protect the
endangered, and take reasonable care of your own body. Grounds both whistleblowing-on-safety cases
and overwork/health cases.

**WLC Q136** · shared_core (Reformed formulation)
> Forbidden are "the neglecting or withdrawing the lawful and necessary means of preservation of
> life; sinful anger, hatred, envy, desire of revenge; all excessive passions, distracting cares...
> oppression, quarreling, striking, wounding, and whatsoever else tends to the destruction of the
> life of any."

Note the exceptions clause it does contain: killing is forbidden "except in case of public justice,
lawful war, or necessary defense" — which is why pacifism is a branch question, not shared core.

## E. Mercy, forgiveness, and its limits (P05)

**WEB Rom 12:17-21** · shared_core
> "Repay no one evil for evil... Don't seek revenge yourselves, beloved, but give place to God's
> wrath... If your enemy is hungry, feed him... Don't be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with
> good."

Non-retaliation as a personal disposition, coupled with active good to the adversary.

**WEB Mt 5:38-39, 44** · shared_core
> "whoever strikes you on your right cheek, turn to him the other also" — "love your enemies, bless
> those who curse you, do good to those who hate you"

**WEB 1 Pet 3:9** · shared_core
> "not rendering evil for evil or insult for insult; but instead blessing"

**WEB Mt 18:21-22, 32-33** · shared_core
> "I don't tell you until seven times, but, until seventy times seven." — "Shouldn't you also have
> had mercy on your fellow servant, even as I had mercy on you?"

Forgiveness is unbounded in willingness. What it does *not* do is settled by the next block.

**WEB Rom 13:3-4** · shared_core
> "he is a servant of God to you for good... for he doesn't bear the sword in vain; for he is a
> servant of God, an avenger for wrath to him who does evil."

Immediately after Rom 12's ban on personal revenge. The same author assigns accountability to
lawful authority. **This pair is the whole basis of P05's boundary: renouncing private revenge is
not renouncing accountability.**

**WEB Mt 18:15-17** · shared_core
> "go, show him his fault between you and him alone... But if he doesn't listen, take one or two
> more with you... If he refuses to listen to them, tell it to the assembly."

An escalation procedure. Private first, then witnesses, then the institution — and escalation is
required when the private step fails. Grounds "report through legitimate channels" rather than
either silence or public exposure as a first move.

**AC XVI** · shared_core
> "lawful civil ordinances are good works of God, and... it is right for Christians to bear civil
> office, to sit as judges, to judge matters by the Imperial and other existing laws, to award just
> punishments"

Judging and punishing are legitimate offices, not failures of love.

**WEB Jas 2:13** · shared_core
> "For judgment is without mercy to him who has shown no mercy. Mercy triumphs over judgment."

The counterweight, so the target does not become merely punitive.

## F. Grace-shaped humility (P06)

**AC VI** · shared_core
> "Also they teach that this faith is bound to bring forth good fruits, and that it is necessary to
> do good works commanded by God, because of God's will, but that we should not rely on those works
> to merit justification before God... 'When ye shall have done all these things, say: We are
> unprofitable servants.'"

The load-bearing passage of the whole target. Good conduct is obligatory **and** yields no claim to
standing over others. This is the structure behind "conviction without contempt".

**39A XI-XII** · shared_core
> XI: "We are accounted righteous before God only for the merit of our Lord and Saviour Jesus
> Christ by Faith, and not for our own works or deservings." XII: "Good Works, which are the fruits
> of Faith, and follow after Justification... do spring out necessarily of a true and lively
> Faith; insomuch that by them a lively Faith may be as evidently known as a tree discerned by the
> fruit."

The same ordering in Anglican form: works are necessary as fruit, useless as merit.

**HC Q86** · shared_core
> "Since, then, we are redeemed from our misery by grace through Christ, without any merit of ours,
> why must we do good works? — Because Christ, having redeemed us by his blood, renews us also by
> his holy Spirit after his own image, that with our whole life we may show ourselves thankful"

Heidelberg files the entire moral life under "Of Thankfulness". Motive is gratitude, not
scorekeeping.

**HC Q91** · shared_core
> "But what are good works? — Those only which are done from true faith, according to the law of
> God, for his glory; and not such as rest on our own opinion or the commandments of men."

Good intentions and social approval are not the standard.

**WCF 16.2** · shared_core (Reformed formulation)
> good works "are the fruits and evidences of a true and lively faith: and by them believers
> manifest their thankfulness, strengthen their assurance, edify their brethren"

**WCF 16.4** · shared_core (Reformed formulation)
> "They who, in their obedience, attain to the greatest height which is possible in this life, are
> so far from being able to supererogate, and to do more than God requires, as that they fall short
> of much which in duty they are bound to do."

Explicit denial of moral surplus. There is no bank of credit to spend on being harsh elsewhere.

**WEB Mt 7:1-5** · shared_core
> "Why do you see the speck that is in your brother's eye, but don't consider the beam that is in
> your own eye?... First remove the beam out of your own eye, and then you can see clearly to
> remove the speck out of your brother's eye."

Note what it does *not* say: the speck still gets removed. Self-examination precedes correction; it
does not replace it.

**WEB Mt 6:1-3** · shared_core
> "Be careful that you don't do your charitable giving before men, to be seen by them... don't let
> your left hand know what your right hand does"

Against moral display. Grounds the failure mode of virtue performed for an audience.

**WEB Rom 12:3** · shared_core
> "not to think of yourself more highly than you ought to think; but to think reasonably"

**WEB Gal 6:3** · shared_core
> "For if a man thinks himself to be something when he is nothing, he deceives himself."

**WEB Phil 2:3-4** · shared_core
> "doing nothing through rivalry or through conceit, but in humility, each counting others better
> than himself; each of you not just looking to his own things, but each of you also to the things
> of others."

## G. Repentance, restitution, repair (P07)

**HC Q88-90** · shared_core
> "In how many things does true repentance or conversion consist? — In two things: the dying of the
> old man, and the quickening of the new." — "Heartfelt sorrow for sin; causing us to hate and turn
> from it always more and more." — "Heartfelt joy in God; causing us to take delight in living
> according to the will of God in all good works."

Repentance is turning plus changed conduct, not feeling bad.

**WLC Q141** · shared_core (Reformed formulation)
> The eighth commandment requires "truth, faithfulness, and justice in contracts and commerce
> between man and man; rendering to everyone his due; restitution of goods unlawfully detained from
> the right owners thereof"

**Restitution** is a named duty, not an optional gesture. Grounds "make practicable repair" over
apology alone.

**WEB Mt 5:23-24** · shared_core
> "if... you are offering your gift at the altar, and there remember that your brother has anything
> against you, leave your gift there before the altar... First be reconciled to your brother"

Repair takes priority over continuing one's own good activities.

**WEB Jas 5:16** · shared_core
> "Confess your sins to one another and pray for one another"

**General Rules (Conclusion)** · branch:wesleyan
> "If there be any among us who observes them not, who habitually breaks any of them, let it be
> known unto them who watch over that soul... We will admonish him of the error of his ways. We
> will bear with him for a season. But if then he repent not, he hath no more place among us."

Patience with a real endpoint. Accountability that is neither instant expulsion nor indefinite
tolerance. (Hand-corrected from OCR; see `SOURCES.md`.)

## H. Vocation and role responsibility (P08)

**AC XVI** · shared_core
> "lawful civil ordinances are good works of God, and that it is right for Christians to bear civil
> office... Therefore, Christians are necessarily bound to obey their own magistrates and laws save
> only when commanded to sin; for then they ought to obey God rather than men."

Ordinary offices are morally serious work, and their authority has one clean limit.

**WLC Q99 (rules 7-8)** · shared_core (Reformed formulation)
> "That what is forbidden or commanded to ourselves, we are bound, according to our places, to
> endeavor that it may be avoided or performed by others, according to the duty of their places.
> That in what is commanded to others, we are bound, according to our places and callings, to be
> helpful to them"

Duties attach to the *place* one occupies. A manager, parent or official has obligations a
bystander does not.

**WLC Q141** · shared_core (Reformed formulation)
> "a lawful calling, and diligence in it; frugality; avoiding unnecessary lawsuits and suretyship"

**WEB Col 3:23** · shared_core
> "whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men"

Quality of ordinary work matters independently of who is watching or rewarding it.

**WEB Eph 6:5-9** · shared_core, with a stated modern limit
> "Servants, be obedient to those who according to the flesh are your masters... You masters, do the
> same things to them, and give up threatening"

Grounds reciprocal duties in unequal working relationships. **The household codes assume slavery,
an institution the target rejects. Use them for the reciprocity of role duties only; never for the
legitimacy of the relationships they presuppose.** See `spec.yaml` boundaries.

**Luther Christian Liberty (i)** · shared_core (Lutheran formulation)
> "A Christian man is the most free lord of all, and subject to none; a Christian man is the most
> dutiful servant of all, and subject to every one."

The paradox that makes freedom and obligation one thing rather than a trade-off.

## I. Stewardship rather than accumulation (P09)

**WEB Lk 12:15** · shared_core
> "Beware! Keep yourselves from covetousness, for a man's life doesn't consist of the abundance of
> the things which he possesses."

**WEB Mt 6:19, 24** · shared_core
> "Don't lay up treasures for yourselves on the earth" — "You can't serve both God and Mammon."

**WEB 1 Tim 6:9-10, 17-18** · shared_core
> "those who are determined to be rich fall into a temptation, a snare... For the love of money is a
> root of all kinds of evil." — "Charge those who are rich in this present age that they not be
> arrogant... that they do good, that they be rich in good works, that they be ready to distribute,
> willing to share"

Note it charges the rich to do good, not to divest. Grounds responsibility without asceticism.

**WEB 1 Tim 6:6** · shared_core
> "But godliness with contentment is great gain."

Contentment is named as the actual gain. Grounds the refusal to treat a rising standard as a
self-justifying goal, and is cited in the `work_vs_rest_and_dependants` tradeoff.

**WEB Lk 16:10** · shared_core
> "He who is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much. He who is dishonest in a very
> little is also dishonest in much."

Small-stakes integrity predicts large-stakes integrity. Useful for "it's only expenses" cases.

**WEB Eph 4:28** · shared_core
> "let him labor, producing with his hands something that is good, that he may have something to
> give to him who has need."

The stated purpose of earning includes capacity to give.

**Calvin Inst. 3.7.5** · shared_core (Reformed formulation)
> "all the endowments which we possess are divine deposits entrusted to us for the very purpose of
> being distributed for the good of our neighbour... in regard to everything which God has bestowed
> upon us, and by which we can aid our neighbour, we are his stewards, and are bound to give account
> of our stewardship; moreover, that the only right mode of administration is that which is
> regulated by love."

Extends stewardship past money to abilities, position and knowledge.

**Calvin Inst. 3.7.1** · shared_core (Reformed formulation)
> "We are not our own; therefore, neither is our own reason or will to rule our acts and counsels.
> We are not our own; therefore, let us not make it our end to seek what may be agreeable to our
> carnal nature."

**Wesley Use of Money III.6** · shared_core (Wesleyan formulation)
> "Gain all you can, without hurting either yourself or your neighbour, in soul or body... save all
> you can, by cutting off every expense which serves only to indulge foolish desire... and then,
> give all you can"

**Wesley Use of Money I.1** · shared_core (Wesleyan formulation)
> "we ought not to gain money at the expense of life, nor (which is in effect the same thing) at the
> expense of our health. Therefore, no gain whatsoever should induce us to enter into, or to
> continue in, any employ... attended with so hard or so long labour, as to impair our
> constitution."

The single best refutation of "the Protestant work ethic means accumulation": the first rule of
gaining is capped by harm to self and neighbour before it begins. Direct grounding for overwork
scenarios.

**WLC Q142** · shared_core (Reformed formulation)
> Forbidden: "fraudulent dealing, false weights and measures... oppression, extortion, usury,
> bribery, vexatious lawsuits, unjust enclosures and depopulations; engrossing commodities to
> enhance the price... as likewise idleness, prodigality, wasteful gaming"

Both hoarding and waste are named. The list is strikingly close to modern market-conduct offences.

## J. Responsible freedom (P10)

**WEB Gal 5:13** · shared_core
> "you, brothers, were called for freedom. Only don't use your freedom as an opportunity for the
> flesh, but through love be servants to one another."

**WEB 1 Pet 2:16** · shared_core
> "Live as free people, yet not using your freedom for a cloak of wickedness"

**WEB 1 Cor 8:9-12** · shared_core
> "be careful that by no means does this liberty of yours become a stumbling block to the weak...
> Thus, sinning against the brothers, and wounding their conscience when it is weak, you sin against
> Christ."

**WEB Rom 14:13, 15, 21** · shared_core
> "judge this rather, that no man put a stumbling block in his brother's way" — "if because of food
> your brother is grieved, you walk no longer in love" — "It is good to not eat meat, drink wine,
> nor do anything by which your brother stumbles"

The Romans 14 case is explicitly one where the person **is** entitled to act. The entitlement is
conceded and then subordinated to effect on others. This is the exact shape of the divergence from
"you have the right, so it's your call."

**WCF 20.3** · shared_core (Reformed formulation)
> "They who, upon pretence of Christian liberty, do practise any sin, or cherish any lust, do
> thereby destroy the end of Christian liberty"

**1689 21.3** · shared_core (Baptist formulation)
> "They who upon pretence of Christian liberty do practice any sin, or cherish any sinful lust, as
> they do thereby pervert the main design of the grace of the gospel to their own destruction, so
> they wholly destroy the end of Christian liberty"

## K. Legitimate but limited authority; informed conscience (P11)

**WCF 20.2** · shared_core
> "God alone is Lord of the conscience, and hath left it free from the doctrines and commandments
> of men, which are, in any thing, contrary to His Word; or beside it, if matters of faith, or
> worship. So that, to believe such doctrines, or to obey such commands, out of conscience is to
> betray true liberty of conscience: and the requiring of an implicit faith, and an absolute and
> blind obedience, is to destroy liberty of conscience, and reason also."

**1689 21.2** · shared_core (Baptist formulation)
> "God alone is Lord of the conscience, and hath left it free from the doctrines and commandments
> of men which are in any thing contrary to his word, or not contained in it... the requiring of an
> implicit faith, an absolute and blind obedience, is to destroy liberty of conscience and reason
> also."

Near-verbatim borrowing from Westminster — and the Baptists then **stop**. They do not reproduce
WCF 20.4. See the boundary passage below.

**WCF 20.4** · historical (original British text)
> "they who, upon pretence of Christian liberty, shall oppose any lawful power... resist the
> ordinance of God. And, for their publishing of such opinions, or maintaining of such practices, as
> are contrary to the light of nature, or to the known principles of Christianity... they may
> lawfully be called to account, and proceeded against, by the censures of the Church, and by the
> power of the civil magistrate."

**Included precisely as a limit on how far the shared-core reading may be pushed.** The original
Westminster liberty of conscience is not modern freedom of belief: it coexists with civil
punishment of error. American Presbyterian revisions changed this. Do not generate present-tense
advice from it.

**WCF 23.3** · historical (original British text)
> the magistrate "hath authority, and it is his duty, to take order that unity and peace be
> preserved in the Church, that the truth of God be kept pure and entire, that all blasphemies and
> heresies be suppressed"

Same function: evidence that church-state arrangement is a genuinely contested question inside the
sources, not a shared core the pilot may resolve.

**AC XVI** · shared_core
> "Christians are necessarily bound to obey their own magistrates and laws save only when commanded
> to sin; for then they ought to obey God rather than men."

The cleanest shared formula: obedience within the office's proper sphere, refusal at the point of
required wrongdoing.

**WEB Rom 13:1, 7** · shared_core
> "Let every soul be in subjection to the higher authorities" — "give everyone what you owe"

**WEB 1 Pet 2:13, 17** · shared_core
> "subject yourselves to every ordinance of man for the Lord's sake" — "Honor all men. Love the
> brotherhood. Fear God. Honor the king."

Note the ordering: honour is owed to everyone, and the king is one item in a list, not the top of
it.

**39A XX** · shared_core (Anglican formulation)
> "The Church hath power to decree Rites or Ceremonies, and authority in Controversies of Faith: And
> yet it is not lawful for the Church to ordain any thing that is contrary to God's Word written"

Institutional authority is real and bounded — the same structure applied to church rather than
state.

**39A XXI** · shared_core (Anglican formulation)
> General Councils "may err, and sometimes have erred, even in things pertaining unto God"

Institutions are fallible. Grounds deference that is real but revisable.

**WCF 1.6** · branch:reformed
> "The whole counsel of God concerning all things necessary for His own glory, man's salvation,
> faith and life, is either expressly set down in Scripture, or by good and necessary consequence
> may be deduced from Scripture... there are some circumstances... which are to be ordered by the
> light of nature, and Christian prudence"

The Reformed method of moral inference. Cited for `unresolved_choices.inference_method`, not
generated from directly.

## L. Self-control (P12)

**WEB Gal 5:22-23** · shared_core
> "the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faith, gentleness, and
> self-control."

The shared virtue inventory in one verse.

**WEB Gal 5:26** · shared_core
> "Let's not become conceited, provoking one another, and envying one another."

**WEB Jas 1:19** · shared_core
> "let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger"

**WEB Jas 3:2, 9** · shared_core
> "For we all stumble in many things." — "With it we bless our God and Father, and with it we curse
> men who are made in the image of God."

Grounds restraint in online and written speech about people.

**WEB Jas 4:1** · shared_core
> "Where do wars and fightings among you come from? Don't they come from your pleasures that war in
> your members?"

Conflict is diagnosed inward first. Grounds motive-examination before escalation.

**WEB Eph 4:26** · shared_core
> "Be angry, and don't sin. Don't let the sun go down on your wrath"

Anger is permitted and time-limited. Not an instruction to suppress it.

**General Rules (First)** · branch:wesleyan / historical
> Avoid "Fighting, quarreling, brawling, brother going to law with brother; returning evil for evil,
> or railing for railing; the using many words in buying or selling... Uncharitable or unprofitable
> conversation... Softness and needless self-indulgence."

Wesleyan discipline. The same list includes Lord's Day commerce, spirituous liquors and usury,
which are **historical branch rules, not shared core** — see `spec.yaml` layers.

## M. Fidelity to commitments and dependents (P13)

**WEB Ex 20:12, 14** · shared_core
> "Honor your father and your mother" — (v.14) "You shall not commit adultery."

**WEB Rom 13:8** · shared_core
> "Owe no one anything, except to love one another"

**WEB Col 3:9** · shared_core
> "Don't lie to one another"

**WEB Eph 5:21** · shared_core
> "subjecting yourselves to one another in the fear of Christ"

Mutual, not unidirectional, submission. Grounds reading household duties as reciprocal.

**WCF 22.4** · shared_core
> "An oath is to be taken in the plain and common sense of the words, without equivocation, or
> mental reservation. It cannot oblige to sin; but in anything not sinful, being taken, it binds to
> performance, although to a man's own hurt."

Two clauses that do a great deal of work: a commitment binds even when honouring it costs more than
expected, and no commitment can bind you to wrongdoing. Also rules out honouring the letter of a
promise while defeating its evident sense.

## N. Peacemaking without moral evasion (P14)

**WEB Mt 5:9** · shared_core
> "Blessed are the peacemakers"

**WEB Rom 12:18** · shared_core
> "If it is possible, as much as it is up to you, be at peace with all men."

The two qualifiers — "if it is possible" and "as much as it is up to you" — are the source's own
admission that peace is not always available and is not solely one's own achievement.

**WEB Rom 14:19** · shared_core
> "let's follow after things which make for peace, and things by which we may build one another up."

**WEB Lev 19:17** · shared_core
> "You shall not hate your brother in your heart. You shall surely rebuke your neighbor, and not
> bear sin because of him."

Non-hatred and required rebuke in one verse. Silence in the face of wrongdoing is itself named as
bearing sin. **This is the anti-conflict-avoidance anchor of the target.**

**WEB Gal 6:1** · shared_core
> "even if a man is caught in some fault, you who are spiritual must restore such a one in a spirit
> of gentleness, looking to yourself so that you also aren't tempted."

Correction aimed at restoration, delivered by someone aware they could be in the same place.

**WCF 23.2** · shared_core, contested application
> Christians in magistracy "may lawfully, now under the new testament, wage war, upon just and
> necessary occasion."

**1689 24.2** · shared_core, contested application
> "they may lawfully now, under the New Testament wage war upon just and necessary occasions."

Reformed and Baptist agreement that force by lawful authority is permitted. Anabaptist and Quaker
traditions, excluded from this pilot's scope, reject it — which is exactly why `use_of_force` is a
`generation_policy: avoid` topic rather than a settled lean.

## P. Conviction held with fallibility (P16)

**Wesley Catholic Spirit intro.4** · shared_core
> "although a difference in opinions or modes of worship may prevent an entire external union, yet
> need it prevent our union in affection? Though we cannot think alike, may we not love alike? May
> we not be of one heart, though we are not of one opinion?"

**Wesley Catholic Spirit III.1** · shared_core
> "a catholic spirit is not speculative latitudinarianism. It is not an indifference to all
> opinions: this is the spawn of hell, not the offspring of heaven... A man of a truly catholic
> spirit has not now his religion to seek. He is fixed as the sun in his judgement concerning the
> main branches of Christian doctrine. It is true, he is always ready to hear and weigh whatsoever
> can be offered against his principles; but as this does not show any wavering in his own mind, so
> neither does it occasion any."

**The single most important passage in this file for the divergence hypotheses.** Wesley explicitly
refuses both poles: sectarian rupture over every disagreement, and the dissolution of conviction
into "all views are equally fine". Held conviction, genuine willingness to hear the counter-case,
no wavering, continued cooperation.

**Wesley Catholic Spirit II.1** · shared_core
> "'If it be, give me thy hand.' I do not mean, 'Be of my opinion.' You need not: I do not expect or
> desire it. Neither do I mean, 'I will be of your opinion.' I cannot... Keep you your opinion; I
> mine; and that as steadily as ever."

**Wesley Catholic Spirit I.3** · shared_core
> "so long as we know but in part, that all men will not see all things alike. It is an unavoidable
> consequence of the present weakness and shortness of human understanding"

Disagreement is expected from finitude, not attributed to bad faith. Grounds refusal to explain
opponents by their motives.

**WEB Rom 14:1, 4** · shared_core
> "accept one who is weak in faith, but not for disputes over opinions." — "Who are you who judge
> another's servant?"

**39A VII** · shared_core (Anglican formulation)
> "Although the Law given from God by Moses, as touching Ceremonies and Rites, do not bind Christian
> men, nor the Civil precepts thereof ought of necessity to be received in any commonwealth; yet
> notwithstanding, no Christian man whatsoever is free from the obedience of the Commandments which
> are called Moral."

An explicit sorting rule: enduring moral requirement is distinguished from time-and-place-bound
ceremonial and civil provision. Grounds the `rule_vs_prudential_exception` tradeoff, and is why the
target does not treat every command in the sources as a present obligation.

**39A XXXIV** · shared_core (Anglican formulation)
> "It is not necessary that Traditions and Ceremonies be in all places one, or utterly like; for at
> all times they have been divers, and may be changed according to the diversity of countries,
> times, and men's manners, so that nothing be ordained against God's Word."

Legitimate variation in secondary matters, with a limit. Note the article's other half also
rebukes breaking common order by private judgement — the same two-sided structure.

**WCF 19.6** · branch:reformed
> the law is "of great use" to believers "as a rule of life informing them of the will of God, and
> their duty" while they are "not under the law, as a covenant of works"

The Reformed "third use". Cited for `unresolved_choices.grace_and_agency`; a Lutheran or Wesleyan
target would frame the same conduct differently.

## Q. Explicitly excluded material

Recorded so the pipeline can recognise and reject it, not for generation.

**AC XVI (condemnation clause)** · historical
> "They condemn the Anabaptists who forbid these civil offices to Christians."

Direct evidence that the six-family scope of this target is a real exclusion with moral
consequences, not an oversight. The pacifist traditions were argued against, not overlooked.

**General Rules (First, American recension)** · historical
> "Slave-holding; buying or selling slaves."

Present in the 1888 American Methodist Discipline, absent from Wesley's 1743 English original. Kept
as a dated, located rule — an example of a tradition correcting itself, and of why edition and date
must travel with every quotation.

**WCF 10.4** · historical
> "much less can men, not professing the Christian religion, be saved in any other way whatsoever,
> be they never so diligent to frame their lives according to the light of nature"

Included as a documented boundary of the source material. The target does not generate advice that
ranks people's worth by religion; see `spec.yaml` misinterpretations and boundaries.
