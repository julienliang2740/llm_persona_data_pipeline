# Basil II (958–1025) — refuse_evidence

Gate run 8 September 2026. **Verdict: `refuse_evidence`.** Not admissible as a persona target.
The acts are richly documented; the reasoning behind them is not, and a persona is built from
reasoning.

## Why this subject is tempting and still fails

Fifty years on the throne, decisive conduct, enormous consequence, a vivid reputation. Fame is not
evidence, and this is the case the gate exists to catch: overwhelming attested **conduct**,
almost no surviving **interiority**.

## Per criterion

| criterion | verdict | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **decisions with reasoning** | **FAIL — binding** | Floor is 6–8 where the act *and* his stated reason survive. Found **one** solid: the novel of 996 against the *dynatoi*, which states its own rationale. Possibly two or three more if the *allelengyon* and the disputed novel carry preambles. Everything else — Kleidion, the 1018 Bulgarian settlement, the Anna–Vladimir marriage, never marrying, the Armenian and Georgian annexations — is act-without-stated-reason. The "reason" in every modern account is a historian's inference or an attribution by Psellos, born 1018 and writing ~40 years posthumously. |
| first-person volume | FAIL | Bounded and small. His share of the fourteen Macedonian land decrees is one major novel plus fragments; the 992 Venice chrysobull survives only in Latin translation; the tomb epitaph is verse of uncertain authorship in later manuscripts; the Menologion he commissioned, not wrote. Well under the ~3,000-word line below which the schema calls a persona mostly invention — and it is chancery product, not personal voice. |
| contestedness | FAIL | The distinctive traits are the disputed ones. "Bulgaroktonos" postdates him by ~150 years; Skylitzes hedges the 15,000 blinding with ὥς φασιν, writing ~75 years after; the figure was reanimated for 19th–20th-c. Greek nationalist politics (Stephenson). For exactly what makes him a distinct persona, the disputed part exceeds the attributable core. |
| domain breadth | PARTIAL | War, fiscal-legal administration, church, diplomacy are real. Family and private life: nothing. |
| testimonial variety | PASS | The one clear strength. Leo the Deacon (contemporary Greek, breaks off ~989/990), Yahya of Antioch (Arabic) and Stephen of Taron (Armenian), both contemporary, against Psellos and Skylitzes later. |

## Acquisition passes actually run

1. **Slot-driven search.** Found Stephenson on the legend, Holmes on the governance, the outline of
   the 996 novel, the Kleidion historiography.
2. **Reference harvesting.** Chased what pass 1 cited, and it **found something pass 1 missed**:
   Eric McGeer, *The Land Legislation of the Macedonian Emperors* (PIMS, 2000) — the first full
   English translation of the fourteen Macedonian decrees, with Svoronos's edition behind it
   (available from the Greek National Research Foundation). Also surfaced J. P. Thomas, "A Disputed
   Novel of Basil II" (*GRBS*), which is itself a contestedness datum: the corpus is small enough
   that single attributions are argued over.
3. **Cross-language** not run to exhaustion. Greek, Arabic, Armenian and Georgian scholarship was
   not searched directly.

**Pass 2 mattered, and that is the point of recording this.** The first gate run on this subject
was a single pass and reached a right answer by a process that had not earned it. The escalation
did not change the verdict, but it changed what the verdict rests on: the corpus is now known to
be *bounded and small* rather than merely *not found*.

## Would `admit_reconstructed` rescue it?

Considered, and no. The circumstance and deeds are well attested and could carry 60%+ of a corpus
honestly. But `admit_reconstructed` declares corpus composition; it does not lower the gate. The
binding criterion asks for decisions with *stated* reasons, and a stated reason cannot be
reconstructed — infer it and you have authored it. A Basil II persona would be a model trained on
a researcher's guesses about why he did things.

## Where a reviewer should reopen this

Only one thing would change the verdict: substantially more surviving legislation in his voice, or
a documentary find. If someone works through McGeer and finds Basil's own preambles carrying
stated reasoning for six or more distinct decisions, this refusal should be reopened.

## Better neighbours

**Michael Psellos** — the *Chronographia*, letters and orations give a large first-person corpus,
and he manoeuvred through nine reigns; the observer of Basil is far better evidenced than Basil.
**Julian** — letters, the *Misopogon*, *Against the Galileans*, dated decisions with stated
reasons, and a genuinely alien value system. **Anna Komnene** — the *Alexiad*, plus a documented
failed conspiracy.
